# OMC16: prompt-conditioned expectations on frozen Bluffer outputs

## Result

The replay completed all 16 GLM-5.3 forecast calls with strict-valid visible outputs. It gives a modest, uneven forecast shift rather than strong support for a pervasive belief that the Bluffer will imitate source-silence boundaries. Every individual predicted probability is below .5. Historical Judge-evaluation exposure raises the equal-family mean forecast from .0373 to .1292, although the old outputs contain zero clear admissions and one unresolved event. These are descriptions of selected frozen answers, not proof that the probability reports are irrational or population-miscalibrated.

The event is narrow: a reply explicitly states that the dossier does not supply the **detail asked about**. Generic hedging, negative claims about another detail and claims that an event did not happen do not automatically count.

## Design and execution

- Four authored SQ28 material families, two original target prompts per family: B0 received neutral archive E0; B1 received authentic historical Judge-evaluation archive E1.
- One previously generated Qwen3.8-max output per family-condition, six answers each. No new Speaker or Reader outputs.
- Two fresh GLM-5.3 forecast calls per family-condition, 16 total. The forecaster sees the exact target prompt and sampling metadata, knows the target lacks the source, and does not see its answers, source, gold or current SQ28 Judge decisions. Historical E1 examples remain visible as part of the target prompt.
- Temperature .5, max_tokens 4096, enabled thinking with low reasoning effort; sequential fresh sessions. See the unchanged [frozen protocol](PROTOCOL_20261008.md).
- Original run `_01` stopped on its first HTTP 403/payment response, with no visible model reply and zero valid forecasts. An identical-prompt recovery `_02` completed 16/16 dispatches, receipts and strict-valid forecasts. The failed request is retained separately: 17 physical requests across both runs, 16 visible model replies. See [credential recovery](RECOVERY_20261008.md).

## Interpretation

A qualitative reading of the visible rationales suggests that the target's instruction to give specific details and avoid reflexive hedging motivates forecasts of continued invention; some B1 rationales additionally invoke successful negative-space behavior in the historical archive. This is an exploratory impression, not a counted rationale-coding endpoint. The measured probabilities show sensitivity to archive exposure while generally favoring absence of the admission event. This is weaker than a claim that the Judge broadly expects sophisticated selective boundary mimicry.

B1 forecasts overpredict admissions on these particular labeled outputs, and produce worse fixed-output Brier scores under either resolution of the one ambiguity. One Actor draw per family-condition does not reveal its true event probability: assigning an absent realized event a nonzero probability is not itself an error of logic. The selected families and hypothesis were chosen after examining SQ28. This retrospective replay neither establishes population calibration nor demonstrates an internal opponent model or a causal explanation of SQ03's identity error. It also differs from SQ28's identity task and its common two-archive Judge background.

The useful finding is a **prompt-conditioned expectation shift with little observed matching behavior in the old sample**. The stronger mechanism hypothesis is not established. No new game, Speaker repetition, R1 replay or sample expansion was launched.
