# Theory v2: opponent-strategy inference and interactive identifiability

This note freezes three results needed for the opponent-strategy experiment. It restates the relevant parts of the formalization in XiaBaiWang_Statistical_ToM_Formalization_20261007.md (especially §§3.2–3.3, 5, 6, and 11) and checks the new Beta–Bernoulli predictions. It is an offline mathematical note, not an empirical result or a claim of a new general theorem.

## 1. Interactive identifiability is a property of the full path law

Let \(K\in\{0,1\}\) have equal prior probabilities. Fix a legal adaptive questioning policy \(\pi\). The policy may select each query and stopping time from the public history, using the same policy kernel under both identity hypotheses. Let \(H^\pi\) be the **entire observed path**: queries, answers, public actions, and stopping (or an agreed fixed-horizon padding). Write

\[
P_k^\pi=\mathcal L(H^\pi\mid K=k),\qquad
\Delta^\pi=\operatorname{TV}(P_0^\pi,P_1^\pi),
\]

where TV is one half of the \(L^1\) distance. If the two path laws are known, the minimum equal-prior error of any possibly randomized decision rule is

\[
R^\star(\pi)=\frac{1-\Delta^\pi}{2}.
\]

Indeed, the optimal rule chooses the larger of the two path densities at each path; its error is one half of their overlap, \(\frac12\int\min(p_0,p_1)=(1-\mathrm{TV})/2\). Thus, under a fixed policy, identical full path laws imply no identity information, while distinct laws permit some improvement over chance. Optimizing over an allowed policy class gives \(\inf_{\pi,d}R(\pi,d)=(1-\sup_\pi\Delta^\pi)/2\), when the decision rule \(d\) is otherwise unrestricted.

The adaptive claim is about \(P_k^\pi\) on complete paths. It does **not** assume a product of independent per-speaker transcript laws: coupled or adaptive questioning can make those transcripts dependent. A factorization is valid only under separately stated conditional-independence assumptions. If the policy, stopping rule, or an auxiliary channel can access \(K\) directly, it is an additional information channel and is outside this setup. Structural equivalence across a policy class means equality of these path laws for every policy in that class; it does not itself promise small finite-sample error when the laws are merely close.

## 2. Joint-source simulation is limited by data processing

Let \(D\) be the true-source law and \(Q\) the independently generated mimic-source law. Under the two identity hypotheses, the ordered source pair \(Z=(S_A,S_B)\) has laws

\[
\mathcal L(Z\mid K=A)=D\otimes Q,\qquad
\mathcal L(Z\mid K=B)=Q\otimes D.
\]

For a fixed legal policy \(\pi\), the **whole interactive record** is generated from that pair by a shared channel \(M_\pi\), which may include adaptive queries, response randomness, and stopping. The channel must not separately reveal whether a source was received or self-generated. Total variation contracts under a common channel, so

\[
\operatorname{TV}(P_A^\pi,P_B^\pi)
\leq \operatorname{TV}(D\otimes Q,Q\otimes D).
\]

If \(\epsilon=\operatorname{TV}(D,Q)\), the source-pair overlap is at least \((1-\epsilon)^2\): for densities \(d,q\),

\[
\min\{d(x)q(y),q(x)d(y)\}
\geq \min\{d(x),q(x)\}\min\{d(y),q(y)\},
\]

and the right side integrates to \((1-\epsilon)^2\). Therefore

\[
\operatorname{TV}(P_A^\pi,P_B^\pi)\leq 2\epsilon-\epsilon^2,\qquad
R^\star(\pi)\geq \frac{(1-\epsilon)^2}{2}.
\]

This is a bound on the complete joint interactive record, not a claim that two adaptively elicited speaker transcripts are independent. At \(Q=D\), the identity label is unidentifiable through this channel, even if the causal receipt histories differed. The result depends on the shared-channel and source-pair assumptions; it does not estimate \(D\), \(Q\), or an LLM's response channel.

## 3. Persistence changes predictive evidence

### 3.1 A fixed latent Bernoulli strategy

Suppose a Reader's four current answers are independent fair bits. A Bluffer draws \(b\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,1)\) once and, conditional on \(b\), answers independently as \(\operatorname{Bern}(b)\). A judge has eight earlier, confirmed Bluffer answers with \(s\) ones. If the same \(b\) persists from that history into the current game, conjugacy gives

\[
b\mid D_s\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1+s,9-s).
\]

For an **ordered, specified** four-bit sequence with \(k\) ones, the posterior predictive probability is

\[
m_s(k)=\frac{\mathrm B(1+s+k,\,9-s+4-k)}{\mathrm B(1+s,\,9-s)}.
\]

There is no binomial coefficient here: this is the probability of one exact sequence, not the probability of any sequence with \(k\) ones. With equal identity priors and current observations \(A=1111, B=0000\), the Reader likelihood \(2^{-4}\) cancels, leaving

\[
P(K=A\mid D_s,A=1111,B=0000)
=\frac{m_s(0)}{m_s(0)+m_s(4)}.
\]

| Historical ones \(s/8\) | \(m_s(0)=P_B(0000\mid D_s)\) | \(m_s(4)=P_B(1111\mid D_s)\) | Exact \(P(K=A\mid\cdot)\) | Posterior-mean plug-in |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1/8 | \(6/13\) | \(1/143\) | \(66/67\approx0.9850746269\) | \(256/257\approx0.9961089494\) |
| 4/8 | \(14/143\) | \(14/143\) | \(1/2\) | \(1/2\) |
| 7/8 | \(1/143\) | \(6/13\) | \(1/67\approx0.0149253731\) | \(1/257\approx0.0038910506\) |

The plug-in column substitutes the posterior mean \(E[b\mid D_s]=(1+s)/10\) before computing the four-answer likelihood. It is not Bayesian predictive integration: generally \(E[(1-b)^4\mid D_s]\ne(1-E[b\mid D_s])^4\). The exact posteriors are symmetric under \(s\leftrightarrow8-s\), and the same fixed current answers support opposite identity attributions as the confirmed history changes.

### 3.2 Refreshed strategy control

If a new independent \(b_{\mathrm{current}}\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,1)\) is drawn for the current game, but held fixed across its four answers, then the eight earlier answers carry no information about it. The current exact-sequence predictions are

\[
P_B(0000)=E[(1-b_{\mathrm{current}})^4]=1/5,\qquad
P_B(1111)=E[b_{\mathrm{current}}^4]=1/5,
\]

so \(P(K=A\mid A=1111,B=0000)=1/2\) for every historical \(s\). The four current answers are still dependent marginally through the shared current-game draw; “refreshed” here means refreshed between games, not between answers.

A simple persistence toy makes the temporal point exact. Let a Bluffer draw \(\theta\sim\operatorname{Bern}(1/2)\) once per \(T\)-answer interaction and repeat \(\theta\), while a Reader emits iid fair bits. The one-answer marginals agree, but the equal-prior optimal role error is \(2^{-T}\). If instead \(\theta\) is refreshed independently on every answer, both speakers have the same full transcript law and the error is \(1/2\) for every \(T\). This toy illustrates dependence structure; it is not an empirical model of LLM strategy.

### 3.3 The \(T^{-1/2}\) Bayes-risk rate and its scope

For a related repeated-answer task, let Reader answers be iid Bernoulli(\(r\)), with known \(r\in(0,1)\). A Bluffer draws \(b\sim\mu\) once per interaction and then answers iid Bernoulli(\(b\)). Assume equal role priors, and that \(\mu\) has no atom at \(r\) and a density that is continuous, finite, and strictly positive in a neighborhood of \(r\). The **prior-averaged Bayes risk** for identifying which of two speakers is the Reader satisfies

\[
R_T^{\mathrm{Bayes}}=\Theta(T^{-1/2}).
\]

For an upper bound, choose the speaker whose empirical rate is closer to \(r\). Conditional on \(b\ne r\), this rule errs only if at least one sample rate deviates from its own mean by a constant fraction of \(|b-r|\), giving a bound \(C\exp[-cT(b-r)^2]\). Integrating this against a locally bounded density gives \(O(T^{-1/2})\); prior mass bounded away from \(r\) contributes exponentially little. For a lower bound, even an oracle told \(b\) has error bounded below by a positive constant when \(|b-r|\leq c_0/\sqrt T\): the two role-swapped product laws then have bounded KL and hence TV bounded away from one. The prior assigns \(\Theta(T^{-1/2})\) mass to that neighborhood. The unknown-\(b\) judge cannot outperform the oracle, giving \(\Omega(T^{-1/2})\). Thus near-null prior mass produces the polynomial rate even after averaging the **oracle-known-\(b\)** risk; the rate is not solely a cost of estimating an unknown parameter.

For \(r=1/2\) and uniform \(b\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,1)\), there is an exact finite-\(T\) formula. If \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(T,1/2)\), then

\[
R_T^\star
=\frac{2\,\mathbb E|N-T/2|}{T+1}
+\frac{\mathbf 1\{T\text{ even}\}}{2(T+1)}P(N=T/2),\qquad
R_T^\star\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi T}}.
\]

The leading constant follows from \(\mathbb E|N-T/2|\sim\sqrt{T/(2\pi)}\); the even-\(T\) tie term is lower order. The accompanying checker compares the exact Bayes error obtained by enumerating all pairs of count classes with this formula for every \(T=1,\ldots,30\).

This is a Bayes average under the specified prior, not a minimax guarantee. If the allowed parameter set contains \(b=r\), Nature can choose \(b=r\), making the two role hypotheses identical and giving minimax error \(1/2\) at every \(T\). A uniform exponential guarantee requires separation from the near-null region (or other additional restrictions). The rate assumes an interior Bernoulli parameter, independent responses conditional on the fixed latent parameter, and the stated local prior regularity; it is not a rate claim for arbitrary language interactions.
