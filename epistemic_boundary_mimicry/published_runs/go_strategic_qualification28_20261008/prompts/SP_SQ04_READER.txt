<source_text>
Between 9 and 14 July 2020, field linguist Arel Vosen recorded elicitation sessions in three villages of the Orvani Ridge. The project focused on how speakers marked the source of information in short event descriptions. It was a small descriptive sample, not a census of the region's speech community. Participants chose their own pseudonyms, which the notebook uses in place of legal names.

Twelve adults completed the same set of 18 picture prompts. Each prompt showed a simple event, such as a cup falling or a gate opening, without printed words. Vosen asked for one sentence per picture and then asked participants to retell four of the scenes as if reporting something they had heard from a neighbor. Responses were recorded on a handheld audio device and transcribed after each session.

In the transcripts, the suffix -ra appeared in 41 of 216 firsthand descriptions and in 9 of 48 neighbor retellings. The particle -mu appeared in 22 neighbor retellings. The notebook treats -ra and -mu as distinct forms; it does not merge them into a single evidential category. Three participants used both forms during the sessions, while the others supplied at least one response without either suffix.

A speaker from Neral village corrected the spelling of one picture label before recording began. Vosen kept that correction in the field log and used the revised label in all later sessions. The notebooks were checked by a second fieldworker, who compared the transcripts against the audio for the first four sessions and marked uncertain syllables for review.

The summary tables group responses by prompt type and form. The report describes the observed distribution within these sessions and leaves broader social or historical explanations for later work.
