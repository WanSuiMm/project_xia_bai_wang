At the Ralden City Museum in 2019, conservator Evi Toren reviewed a cleaning trial on 18 bronze register weights removed from a decommissioned tide clock. The pieces all had shallow engraved numbers; the numbers were used to track them, not to judge whether a weight remained suitable for use. The trial compared two dry cleaning pads and one soft-brush protocol.

Before treatment, an assistant photographed each numbered weight beside a grey reference strip. The team divided the pieces into three groups of six, with each protocol assigned to one group. For every piece, the operator made four passes using the assigned tool, collecting material from each pass on a fresh labeled paper square. Each square was weighed immediately before and after that pass to estimate collected loose residue.

The first-pass record lists median residue masses of 14 milligrams for the blue pad, 11 milligrams for the cream pad, and 13 milligrams for the brush. By the fourth pass, each group's median per-pass residue mass was lower than its first-pass median, although the note cautions that this measures material transferred to the collection surface rather than all corrosion removed from the object. Two pieces from the blue-pad group were reviewed again because their engraved digits were less legible in the final photographs.

Toren's summary says the cream pad produced the lowest median on the paper-square measure after the first pass. It recommends retaining the labels and photographs with the collection record before any later treatment decision. The trial ran across two afternoons, and a second conservator initialed the final table after checking the arithmetic against the weighing slips.

The report treats its result as a comparison among these 18 pieces under this protocol. Each group kept the same numbered members throughout, and no weight was returned to active use during the trial.
