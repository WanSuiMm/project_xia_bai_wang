During the 2021 survey season, ecologist Ruvan Elsen coordinated evening counts of the lantern marsh frog around the fictional Selnor Basin. The survey compared three shore zones in a protected wetland; it did not estimate the total number of animals living in the basin. Observers recorded calls and visible adults as separate event types.

Six fixed listening stations were visited on eight evenings between May 3 and May 18. At each station, an observer waited until 35 minutes after sunset, then recorded for 9 minutes from a marked platform. A second observer handled the field sheet, noting cloud cover and whether a passing vehicle interrupted the interval. A disturbed interval was repeated once at the end of that evening.

The eastern sedge zone produced 42 frog-call events across the survey, while the west pool edge produced 28. These were event totals, not unique callers: the same frog could call on more than one evening. The central willow zone had 31 events. Visible adults were noted on five survey evenings, but they were not individually marked, and the note keeps those sightings separate from the call count.

Two of the six stations had interrupted intervals. Both repeats were included in the totals after the original interrupted segments were crossed out in the field sheets. Elsen reviewed the eight sheets with a reserve technician and entered a short code beside each accepted interval. The code records whether the sound was assigned to the lantern marsh frog or left unidentified.

The field report describes the station layout, timing, and event totals as an early-season index for comparing zones during those eight evenings. It gives no estimate for other months or years. The team used handheld recorders and field sheets, and the reserve retained the audio files with the survey folder.
