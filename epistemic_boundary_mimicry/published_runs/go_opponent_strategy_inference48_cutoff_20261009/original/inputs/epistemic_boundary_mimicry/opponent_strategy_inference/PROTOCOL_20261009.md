# OSI48: Opponent Strategy Inference and Epistemic Attribution

Authorized 2026-10-09. This is a prospective, fixed-stimulus Judge experiment, with a fully disclosed model family and an unknown scalar opponent parameter. It tests history-dependent attribution and sensitivity to the history/current parameter coupling. It does not generate LLM Speakers or test spontaneous strategic adaptation, an unknown model family, naturalistic interrogation, or general Theory of Mind.

## Question, intervention and exact mechanism

Can a Judge use identity-confirmed opponent history when it predicts the current opponent, and disregard that history when the current parameter is independently refreshed?

Exactly one current participant is the Reader. Its seat K is an independent fair A/B draw. Its private source contains four independent fair binary records, and it reports them faithfully. The other participant has no source and reports four binary answers. Conditional on a latent b, these answers are independent Bernoulli(b). The current b is held fixed for all four current answers. Source, seat, and opponent randomness are independent except for the explicit coupling below. Both participants have identical deterministic formatting.

The archive contains eight answers from an identity-confirmed non-reader batch. All eight archive answers share one b_archive drawn uniformly from [0,1]. This archive has no seat/name mapping to current A/B. Under either identity hypothesis it describes the current non-reader's generator, not a particular visible seat.

- PERSISTENT: b_current = b_archive.
- REFRESHED: b_current is a new uniform draw, independent of b_archive, its eight outcomes, the source and the seat. It is drawn once per current trial, not once per answer.

The history count s is 1, 4, or 7. The chronological histories, fixed before calls, are 00100000, 01101001, and 11011111 respectively. The current observed packet is A=1111/B=0000, or its seat swap. Exactly these positive-probability observations are selected for this controlled stimulus grid. No identity label is sampled for scoring, and packet selection supplies no additional identity clue.

Let alpha=1+s and beta=9-s. Under PERSISTENT, b given the archive has Beta(alpha,beta) distribution. Write (x)_4=x(x+1)(x+2)(x+3). Since the Reader likelihood is 1/16 for either four-bit extreme:

    p*(Reader is the 1111 seat) = (beta)_4 / [(alpha)_4 + (beta)_4].

For s=1,4,7 this is 66/67, 1/2, 1/67. Under REFRESHED the non-reader likelihood of each extreme is 1/5, giving 1/2 in every case. Seat swapping complements p_A. All observations have positive likelihood under both identity hypotheses.

The parameter's posterior and computed identity posterior are host-only. Prompts disclose the prior, sampling rules, eight actual archive answers, current answers, and costs, but no posterior formula, likelihood table, computed probabilities, expected decisions, other conditions, or worked example.

## Frozen finite design and randomization

Three history counts x two coupling mechanisms x two seat orders x two Judge models x two independent calls = 48 provider requests. Models are glm-5.3 and qwen3.8-max. There are 12 unique visible prompts, each sent twice independently to each model. Repeats use byte-identical prompts and fresh sessions; schedule and request IDs are withheld. The exact same archive ordering is used across coupling, seat, model and repeat conditions.

The first scheduled experimental call also serves as the real interface check; no extra paid smoke call is authorized.

These are selected finite stimuli, not 48 iid games or source families. History count, seat swap and repeated calls are related design cells. Report models separately. Two repeats describe observed call variation; they do not establish a population variance or broad reliability estimate.

## Reports and registered continuous endpoints

Output one JSON object with exactly p_A, decision, reason. p_A must be finite and in [0,1]; decision is A, B, or ABSTAIN; reason is nonempty and at most 80 English words. Costs: correct 0, incorrect 1, abstain 0.25. The optimal action for a given posterior is A above .75, B below .25, and ABSTAIN on [.25,.75], including ties.

Primary quantities, by model:

1. Equal-stimulus mean posterior squared error, separately for PERSISTENT and REFRESHED. The weighting is the registered stimulus grid, not a generative population average.
2. History reversal after converting reports to the probability that the 1111 seat is Reader: Delta_c = mean(p_1111 | s=1,c) - mean(p_1111 | s=7,c), averaging seats/repeats. Targets are Delta_P=65/67 and Delta_R=0. Report their difference, target 65/67, and the three count-specific means. Monotonicity alone is insufficient.
3. REFRESHED historical leakage: squared distance from .5 and its observed history contrast. This is a behavior measure, not proof of an internal causal mechanism.

Secondary: mean/max absolute posterior error; expected decision loss and excess above min(p*,1-p*,.25); consistency of the action with the reported probability; seat-complement residual; and descriptive within-cell variance/mean-bias decomposition using the two frozen repeats. Reports within 1e-4 of p* are counted solely as a rounding-precision summary, not a success gate.

Also compare squared distance to the posterior-mean plug-in comparator. Plugging E[b|archive] into four independent likelihoods instead of integrating gives .9961089494,.5,.0038910506 for the unswapped PERSISTENT packet. It yields the same action directions. This offline comparator distinguishes numerical predictive integration from mere reversal; matching either output does not identify an internal algorithm.

No arbitrary all-or-nothing scientific PASS/FAIL threshold is introduced. Runtime validity and completeness are separate. A model/coupling aggregate or paired contrast is complete only when all of its registered cells have valid observations. Missing/invalid cells remain missing; no score is renormalized into a complete primary endpoint. Retain all visible attempts regardless of answer correctness.
